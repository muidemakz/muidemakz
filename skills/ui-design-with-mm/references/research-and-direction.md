# Research and visual direction

The full text of SKILL.md section 3, including the visual direction gate. Read when research begins; SKILL.md carries the short form.

When a live page, URL, or existing file is the stated source of truth, read [source fidelity](source-fidelity.md) and record the source inventory (sections, measured controls, typography, icons, layout) before foundations. Research before creating or changing foundations/tokens, including for inherited systems. Use current web research, actual product examples, and supplied screenshots, links, and assets. Research broadly for a new build and narrowly around the affected decisions for an edit.

Use two complementary research tracks when the existing brand or product system does not already determine the direction: product and interaction evidence, plus visual reference exploration. For visual exploration, use relevant publicly accessible sources such as Mobbin, Behance, Dribbble, Pinterest, Awwwards, product galleries, or comparable showcases when they can add useful evidence. Do not browse every platform by default or treat showcase work as proof that a pattern improves UX. Choose a small relevant set and record the direct link, observation, inference, proposed application, and what will not be copied. Keep visual inspiration separate from product facts and accessibility evidence. If the user is composing a design from elements of several inspirations, follow [inspiration composition](inspiration-composition.md): record the elements chosen, translate them into the user's theme through tokens, and get the composition ledger approved before foundations. This is a method inside make design and make prototype, not a separate intent.

Research the user's actual end-to-end workflow, including repeat use and adjacent tools that solve a relevant step. Do not limit research to direct competitors: a PDF upload, conversion, or file-management product such as iLovePDF may inform an import workflow even when it is not a product competitor. Select references by the task or interaction being studied, explain why each is relevant, and do not transfer patterns across contexts without evidence. Where [journey and trust research](journey-trust-and-requirements.md) applies to this run, capture product requirements plus first-use, returning-use, loading, decision, editing, recovery, and completion states before screen design.

Read [research sources](research-sources.md) when the owner has supplied its curated list, or when you need its format for recording a source. The list is an owner-supplied placeholder; do not populate it yourself or present it as complete, and continue with brief-specific research while it is empty. If research access or essential evidence is missing, state the gap and request accessible sources; do not substitute invented findings.

Record concise findings with source links and their implications for this product. Separate observation from inference. Validate inherited choices without treating research as permission to redesign unrelated areas. Trace design decisions to the brief, research, or an approved assumption.

Plan copy around user tasks and the product's terminology. Draft functional UI labels and clearly marked placeholder copy using the brief and relevant research. Identify placeholders in the delivered artifact or adjacent annotations. Do not invent factual claims, testimonials, statistics, or shipped capabilities. Route unresolved copy assumptions through approval.

Run the [checkpoint and continuation](../SKILL.md#checkpoint-and-continuation) protocol before moving to foundations and tokens.

Before the first polished visual output, use the **visual direction gate**. When existing brand evidence or an established product system does not already determine the direction, present 2-3 evidence-based visual directions with their implications, tradeoffs, and one recommendation. Each direction must describe an actual visual system—typography, color behavior, type scale, spacing rhythm, composition, surface and component treatment, icon and illustration approach, motion, and patterns deliberately excluded—not merely a layout or density variation. State for each direction the one idea that makes it recognizable, grounded in the brand or research; for an inherited brand system it is the brand's own distinctive trait, not an invention. Tie the direction to the research ledger and state what remains provisional. Ask the user to choose a direction, approve the recommendation, or request targeted questions. Use native selectable options when available and a copyable fallback otherwise. Do not ask for a redundant direction choice when the inherited brand system is decisive, or when the user's approved composition ledger and theme already determine the direction. Treat the choice as run-specific unless the user explicitly asks to save it as a recurring preference. Do not create polished deliverable screens while this gate is pending. Do show each direction as a real preview of the deliverable's key screen in its own content, as set out in [direction previews](direction-previews.md), because a description alone is not enough to choose from. Offer at most three directions, recommend one and pre-select it, and ask one decision per message. When the user asks for a better direction for something that already exists, show the current design as one of the options.

Read [visual quality checks](visual-quality.md) before the first polished visual output. Apply them during composition and at delivery; they make production quality observable rather than a claim. Design each state around one clear primary task or decision; keep secondary information available through progressive disclosure.
