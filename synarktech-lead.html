Leading design across three AI products

At Synarktech I led design across three products: PlayHour, AlphaBuzz and TLQ. PlayHour was built to let people in an organisation create and play games together. AlphaBuzz was an AI-assisted product for people deciding when to buy and sell crypto. I led a small team of product designers and a brand designer, set the daily rhythm, reviewed the work and took it to stakeholders so decisions kept moving. I also led the branding for all three. Three products, one small team

New ground

The products were built when AI products were just starting to appear. PlayHour had to make creating a game easy for a team, and AlphaBuzz had to help people act on an AI forecast, so there were few settled patterns to borrow.

Few people, many decisions

Three products moved at once with a small team. Without a clear rhythm, work would stall waiting for decisions, or ship without anyone senior having looked at it.

How the team ran

The same four steps, every day, across all three products.

  1. 01 Daily Stand-Up The team met every day to say what was done, what was blocked and what was next.
  2. 02 I Review The Work I reviewed what the designers produced before it went any further.
  3. 03 Share And Decide I shared it with stakeholders, and we decided what to move on to next.
  4. 04 Write It Down Decisions and progress were documented in Linear, so nothing lived only in someone’s head.

Where my input changed the outcome

AlphaBuzz

The forecast cards were meant to show a signal. From further research I recommended adding a percentage that says how certain the forecast was, which was not in the original plan. Someone deciding to buy or sell should know how sure the forecast is, not only what it says.

PlayHour

We started with one direction and moved to a better one after my input. Add: what the original direction was, what it became, and the screens before and after.

The One Decision

I chose to show how certain each AlphaBuzz forecast was, even though a bare signal looks more decisive. A percentage can make a call feel less sure, but someone about to put money behind it deserves to know how much to trust it. I would rather the product be honest about its own confidence than look more certain than it is.

Reflection

Leading a small team is mostly making decisions visible. A daily rhythm, a written record and one person reviewing everything meant stakeholders could see what was happening and the designers knew where they stood. The AlphaBuzz change reminded me that a lead’s best contribution is sometimes a question the team has not asked yet, such as how sure we are.
