Read 1: AI First Read

  • After you submit

    The form says a team member will be in touch, but not what that involves or how long approval takes.

  • Who qualifies

    Nothing says if you need proof of income, a bank account or a credit history.

  • Deposits

    ₦25,000 to ₦45,000, with no word on refunds or missed payments.

Another run also flagged the sums: ₦10,000 a week for 26 weeks plus ₦45,000 down is ₦305,000, with no cash price to compare.

Redesign

The same skill updated the design from the findings, on a separate AFTER page. The rebuilt original is untouched.

What Changed

  • Total cost is visible

    Each card shows the pay-small-small (installment) total, with the pay-in-full option beside it. Installments stay first.

  • Pay in full

    Buy outright runs through cart, checkout and success as a prototype. No payment is taken.

  • Prices on the card

    Four phones had their financing line hidden in "Details". It now sits on the card face.

  • Forms

    Visible labels, error and submitted states, and a line on what happens next. Field border contrast went from 1.46:1 to between 5.3:1 and 5.8:1, where 3:1 is the accessibility minimum.

  • Buttons

    In my own check, none of the 66 tap targets was left unwired. Buttons scroll to the form and show feedback. Valideity's After run still reported the ⓘ next to EasyBuy doing nothing for 3 of 10 simulated shoppers.

How The Method Shaped It

  • Copy from the live page

    Anything unconfirmed is marked "open question", not invented.

  • Everything is a component

    14 cards come from 1 component and 4 forms from 1, so one change updates every card or form.

  • Nothing is hard-coded

    Every text style, colour, border, corner and spacing value comes from a design token, with none typed in by hand, so real prices drop in without a redesign.

  • Inside what was approved

    Sections keep their order, installments stay first, and each new screen was agreed first.

Prompts

Redesign, prompt by prompt

10 of 10 prompts

The core redesign took 10 prompts in about 2.5 hours. These are my own words, shortened, with what each one produced.

  1. 0 min

    "Use /ui-design-with-mm. Edit and update the existing Sun King smartphone designs in Figma… treat the Valideity study as a lead, not a measurement."

    Findings triaged. Page duplicated as AFTER.

  2. 15 min

    "Approve all choices A to I with the recommendations. Pace: Procedural."

    One product card and one form, built as components.

  3. 26 min

    "Approve J: show each of the four phones' confirmed financing line on the card face, verbatim."

    Four cards show their financing line.

  4. 48 min

    "Round 2 on the AFTER page: the outright purchase path. Reuse the Round 1 components; do not rebuild them."

    Cart, drawer, checkout and success planned.

  5. 52 min

    "Approve the Round 2 plan with the recommended options 1-9, and continue Round 1 wiring."

    Cart path built. Every control wired.

  6. 91 min

    "Op is Opay. Prices are fine."

    Open question closed. Prices confirmed for the design.

  7. 116 min

    "Can we try this for the More smartphone cards… you can hide things in details."

    Compact price comparison on the card.

  8. 122 min

    "1.b, use the same logic, assume a lesser price."

    Assumed prices, marked as assumptions.

  9. 137 min

    "Yes, add it, but do the other More smartphones too. Make sure the details match for each."

    All four cards match.

  10. 144 min

    "Cart holds one phone line at a time (quantity 1-3); adding a different phone replaces it. Wire Buy outright on all four, plus EZ 3 and EZ 3 Pro."

    Buy outright works on all six phones.

16 more prompts went on polish: form fills, the verify tick, card spacing and hierarchy.

What The Two Figma Runs Found

Same task, 10 simulated sessions each: the rebuilt original scored 76 and the redesign 86. The top finding in both was the enquiry confirmation. It recorded no chosen plan or next steps in the Before (7 of 10), and in the After 9 of 10 still said it was missing plan details, so that is the next fix. Also flagged in the After: phone not preselected (6 of 10) and no phone name on the form (5 of 10). Buy outright exists only in the After, and none of its findings concern it, so I cannot say how shoppers would use it.

What This Does Not Show

It is a pointer, not proof. Ten sessions per version and five personas, all simulated. No real customer took part.

Next

Make the confirmation repeat the chosen plan, then test with real Sun King customers, including the Buy outright path, which only the After has and these findings do not cover.

Conclusion

The research kept finding the same friction: people could not see what a phone would cost, and the path from a card to a finished enquiry gave them no feedback. The redesign addresses both without touching Sun King’s model. Installments stay the main path, and the total and the pay-in-full price sit on every card, so no one does the sums. The score moved from 76 to 86, but the After run still found the confirmation missing plan details for 9 of 10 simulated shoppers, so that is the next thing to fix.
